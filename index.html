Simran Jot Kaur — Benchmarking NeRF Variants on Sparse-View Reconstruction

Problem Summary

Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) are powerful tools for synthesizing novel views of a scene. However, they typically require a large number of input images. In many practical applications like AR/VR, robotics, and medical imaging, we often only have access to a few views. This project benchmarks NeRF variants designed to operate effectively under such sparse-view constraints.

Previous Work

  • PixelNeRF: Introduces scene-agnostic priors using CNN-based feature extraction.
  • SparseNeRF: Utilizes self-supervised geometric consistency via depth ranking.
  • S3NeRF: Leverages multi-view feature fusion and scene-specific training.
  • ZeroRF: A Deep Image Prior method that trains per-scene without pretraining.

Description of Work

We implemented and evaluated four NeRF models under sparse-view constraints (1, 3, and 9 input views) across two datasets: NeRF-Synthetic and DTU. Metrics used include PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and MAE.

Models were tested for generalization, geometric fidelity, perceptual quality, and computational efficiency.

Results

  • PixelNeRF: Best generalization on unseen scenes; performance drops in 1-view settings.
  • SparseNeRF: Excellent geometry retention from 1–3 views, especially in occluded regions.
  • S3NeRF: Best perceptual and structural quality; more memory-intensive.
  • ZeroRF: Works well with 3+ views but slower and less detailed under severe sparsity.

Analysis of Work

New Results

This project presents the first direct benchmark comparison across four NeRF variants on sparse inputs under unified conditions. It highlights the individual strengths and limitations of each approach.

Meeting Goals

We achieved our goals of comparing performance across sparse scenarios, identifying suitable models per use case, and proposing a hybrid design for future work.

Future Work

We propose a hybrid architecture combining:

  • PixelNeRF’s encoder for generalization
  • S3NeRF’s feature fusion for robust view integration
  • SparseNeRF’s geometric constraints for stability
  • ZeroRF’s optimization scheme for flexibility

This hybrid model could be trained with photometric and perceptual losses for higher-quality reconstructions from as few as one image.
