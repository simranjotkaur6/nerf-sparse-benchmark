Benchmarking NeRF Variants on Sparse-View Reconstruction

Problem Summary

Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) have emerged as state-of-the-art methods for novel view synthesis. However, they depend on dense image inputs with known camera poses, which limits their usability in real-world scenarios such as AR/VR applications, mobile robotics, and medical imaging—domains where collecting hundreds of calibrated images is infeasible. In sparse-view settings, NeRFs often suffer from:

  • Shape-radiance ambiguity: Multiple plausible shapes could explain the few views.
  • Lack of geometric constraints: Especially in occluded or textureless regions.
  • Overfitting: Instead of generalizing, models memorize the few seen views.

This project benchmarks several NeRF variants specifically designed to address sparse input constraints.

Previous Work

  • PixelNeRF: Introduces scene-agnostic priors using a CNN encoder and pixel-aligned features. It generalizes well to unseen scenes but degrades with extreme sparsity.
  • SparseNeRF: Introduces geometric consistency via depth ranking losses between view projections. This improves 3D stability without needing ground-truth depth.
  • S3NeRF: Enhances robustness with multi-view feature fusion (e.g., attention modules), achieving best-in-class reconstruction in 1–3 view settings at the cost of higher memory usage.
  • ZeroRF: Uses a Deep Image Prior strategy to optimize a randomly initialized MLP per scene. This needs no pretraining but converges slower and yields lower fidelity in complex scenes.

Description of Work

We implemented and benchmarked the above four models on two datasets:

  • NeRF-Synthetic: Clean, synthetic scenes with known geometry and fixed lighting.
  • DTU: Real-world scans with complex geometry and large camera motions.

We tested each model under three sparse-view configurations: 1, 3, and 9 input views.

Evaluation metrics used:

  • PSNR: Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio for pixel accuracy.
  • SSIM: Structural Similarity Index for perceptual structure.
  • LPIPS: Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity for visual fidelity.
  • MAE: Pose estimation error, where applicable.

Results

Findings per model:

  • PixelNeRF works best in scenes with texture and when 5+ views are available. Its CNN encoder allows for generalization, but performance drops with fewer views.
  • SparseNeRF excels at preserving geometric structure from as little as one image. Its depth-based constraints help maintain consistent shape under occlusion.
  • S3NeRF yields the highest quality results even with just 1–3 views, thanks to its attention-based fusion of multiple views and strong scene-specific priors.
  • ZeroRF offers a flexible fallback for domains lacking training data, though it's computationally heavy and prone to blurry reconstructions with extreme sparsity.

Analysis of Work

New Results

We provide the first unified benchmark of these models under consistent sparse-view settings. Our analysis uncovers the specific strengths and limitations of each approach across dataset types.

Meeting Goals

All primary goals were met:

  • Comparing model performance under 1–9 views
  • Evaluating quality using multiple perceptual and structural metrics
  • Proposing a hybrid NeRF architecture

Future Work

We propose a hybrid model that combines:

  • Encoder: Scene-agnostic CNN (PixelNeRF)
  • Fusion: Cross-view attention (S3NeRF)
  • Backbone: Factorized MLP with Deep Image Prior (ZeroRF)
  • Geometry: Depth ranking and smoothness regularization (SparseNeRF)

Training this hybrid model with both photometric and perceptual loss functions is expected to provide high-fidelity reconstructions with only 1–3 input views.

References

  1. Alex Yu, Vickie Ye, Matthew Tancik, Angjoo Kanazawa.
    PixelNeRF: Neural Radiance Fields from One or Few Images. CVPR 2021.
    PixelNeRF learns a scene-agnostic prior using a fully convolutional NeRF architecture that enables single or few-shot novel view synthesis without 3D supervision. It generalizes across scenes and categories on datasets like ShapeNet and DTU.
  2. Guangcong Wang, Zhaoxi Chen, Chen Change Loy, Ziwei Liu.
    SparseNeRF: Distilling Depth Ranking for Few-shot Novel View Synthesis. arXiv:2311.10902 [cs.CV], 2023.
    SparseNeRF uses coarse or noisy depth maps from consumer sensors and applies depth ranking and spatial continuity constraints to enable few-shot novel view synthesis. It shows strong performance on LLFF, DTU, and the new NVS-RGBD dataset.
  3. Wenqi Yang, Guanying Chen, Chaofeng Chen, Zhenfang Chen, Kwan-Yee K. Wong.
    S3-NeRF: Neural Reflectance Field from Shading and Shadow under a Single Viewpoint. arXiv:2305.07085 [cs.CV], 2023.
    This method reconstructs full 3D geometry and BRDFs from single-view images lit under varying point lights. It leverages shading and shadows instead of multi-view photo-consistency and supports applications like relighting and novel view synthesis.
  4. Ruoxi Shi, Xinyue Wei, Cheng Wang, Hao Su.
    ZeroRF: Fast Sparse View 360° Reconstruction with Zero Pretraining. arXiv:2312.09249 [cs.CV], 2023.
    ZeroRF integrates a Deep Image Prior into a factorized NeRF framework to enable fast and generalizable 360° reconstruction from sparse views without pretraining. It achieves SOTA results on multiple sparse-view benchmarks.
